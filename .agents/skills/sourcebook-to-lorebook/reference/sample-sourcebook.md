## 1. Running the Game

**Tone & mood.** The Drowned Crown is grim and low-fantasy. The tide is always coming in. Consequences bite; hope is scarce but earned when it arrives. Favor unease over spectacle.

**Fiction inspirations.** The First Law meets The Terror. Coastal rot, failing empires, debts that outlive the people who owed them.

**Narration style.** Tight third-person, sensory-first. Lead with what characters smell, hear, and feel before what they see. Let silence do work; cut a scene the moment the decision is made, not when the description is done.

**Turn procedure.**

1. **Declare intent** — the player states what they want and how they approach it.
2. **State the approach's cost or risk** — name what's at stake before rolling.
3. **Resolve** — roll only when the outcome is uncertain *and* the stakes matter. Outcomes: full success, partial success (success with a cost), or failure-with-consequence. Never a flat "nothing happens."
4. **Narrate the consequence** — make failure interesting and forward-moving; a failed roll changes the situation.
5. **Advance the clock** — move time, weather, or the tide. The world does not wait.

**Pacing.** Front-load each scene with a concrete threat or unanswered question. When the central decision of a scene resolves, cut.

**NPC portrayal.** Voice NPCs with their own wants and limits. They lie, stall, and remember. Never speak or decide for a player's character.

## 2. The World: Saltmere

Saltmere is a sinking coastal city built on reclaimed marshland and older ruins. The ruling House Vane clings to power through debt and salt-rotted fortifications. The harbor district, Saltbottom, floods twice daily and is ruled by the Tide Court — smugglers and moneylenders. The eastern Reach is abandoned save for the Drowned Chapel, where the Crowned are said to lair. Fog rolls in from the Greymare Sea most evenings.

## 3. The Drowned Crown (creature)

The Crowned are drowned corpses reanimated by the tide and crowned in rusted salvage — chains, coins, broken blades fused to their skulls. They rise from the Greymare during thick fog and are drawn to the living warmth. They do not speak. A Crowned is slow but relentless; in numbers they drag victims beneath the surf. **Encounter rule:** when a character encounters a Crowned, roll Resolve vs. the Crowned's Dread (3). A failure means the character freezes or is seized; a partial success means they break free at a cost (lost gear, a wound, the tide rising).

## 4. Magic & Mistrust

Magic in Saltmere is rare, fading, and feared. Working magic visibly draws the Crowned and the ire of common folk. Most "magic" is charlatanry, cold-reading, or old rituals half-remembered. True sorcerers hide what they are. The Tide Court pays well for confirmed mages — delivered alive, usually.
